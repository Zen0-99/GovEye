# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

android

## Users

UK citizens who want to follow what Parliament and government are actually doing, skewing
millennial / Gen Z / Gen A. They reach the app on a phone, usually in short sessions, often
without prior knowledge of parliamentary procedure. Their job is to stay informed about
specific MPs, parties, and topics they care about without reading Hansard.

## Product Purpose

GovEye is a tracking app for UK government activity — the "FotMob of Government". It pulls
divisions (votes), government publications, written statements, legislation, MP speeches, and
MPs' registered financial interests into one chronological feed, and lets users follow specific
MPs, parties, and topics so the firehose becomes a personal feed.

Success is a user opening the app and understanding, in seconds, what happened and whether it
touches anything they follow.

## Positioning

Two claims that neighboring products cannot truthfully copy:

1. **Modern, mobile-native access.** The same public data as TheyWorkForYou, Hansard, and
   parliament.uk, but built phone-first and genuinely pleasant to scan. The incumbents are
   desktop-first, slow, and visually dated. This is the primary differentiator.
2. **Personal tracking.** Following MPs, parties, and topics turns a chronological firehose
   into a feed about people and subjects the user already cares about.

Jargon translation is an aspiration, not a current capability. Topic tags (derived at build
time by pattern-matching source text) are the current best tool for making opaque titles
comprehensible, and they demonstrably help. Do not claim plain-language summarisation the
product does not perform.

## Operating Context

- Short, frequent phone sessions; feed sorted newest-first.
- The dominant job is chronological catch-up ("what happened since I last looked"), because
  recency drives the sort. Personal relevance (followed MPs / tags / parties) and topical
  self-interest are real secondary jobs layered on top of that ordering.
- Onboarding captures topic tags, which seed the feed's initial tag filter.
- Recess periods are a real state: Parliament stops sitting and the feed legitimately empties.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Feed item types**, each with genuinely different content shape:

| Type | Distinct content |
|---|---|
| Division | Title, house (Commons/Lords), Aye/No counts, result bar, votes of followed MPs |
| Publication | Title, issuing organisation, optional image URL |
| Statement | Title, member role ("by …"), answering body |
| Legislation | Title (heavily legalistic), formatted legislation type |
| MP vote | Followed MP's Aye/No on a division, with avatar and party colour |
| Speech | Followed MP's speech text from a debate, with avatar |
| Financial | Followed MP's income/expense entry, amount, category, counterparty |

- Every item type carries topic tags with a `hitCount` (mention count), ordered descending.
- Titles for legislation and many divisions are legalese and frequently unintelligible alone.
- Data is local-first (Room, bundled seed DB with a patch/version update system); no live API
  calls on the feed path.
- Stack: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3, Hilt, Room. Multi-module (`app`, `core:ui`,
  `core:data`, `core:domain`).
- Party colours exist as real data (`LocalPartyAccent`) and are meaningful.
- Vote colours (Aye / No) are semantic and theme-aware (`VoteColors`).

## Brand Commitments

- Name: GovEye.
- The app ships a single restrained, near-monochromatic colour scheme ("Sky") in light, dark,
  and AMOLED variants, built on Material 3 colour roles. Accents are muted blue-grey;
  `withSingleAccent()` collapses secondary and tertiary roles onto primary.
- Colour is reserved for meaning — vote results, party identity, followed-MP highlights — and
  is not spent on decorative per-type coding. This is a deliberate, repeatedly reaffirmed
  decision, not an accident.
- Explicit anti-goal: anything that reads as templated or "basic AI design" — arbitrary
  colour-coded squares, generic icon badges, decorative chrome unattached to data.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real bundled dataset: divisions with real Aye/No counts, real publications from named
  government organisations, real written statements, real legislation records, real MP
  financial interests, real speech text, real MP photos and party affiliations.
- Real topic tags with real mention counts, generated by `build_tags.py` in the companion
  `goveye-data` repository.
- Typical feed load: ~17 divisions, ~5 publications, ~10 statements, ~8 legislation, ~20
  financial, ~100 speeches, ~119 MP votes across ~27 date groups.
- No testimonials, user counts, press, or commercial claims exist. Do not fabricate any.

## Product Principles

1. **Recency is the spine.** The feed is chronological; any structure must survive newest-first
   ordering and mixed item types interleaved by date.
2. **Colour carries meaning or it does not appear.** Vote outcome, party, and followed-MP
   status may use colour. Source type may not.
3. **Differentiate by content, not decoration.** Item types already differ in what data they
   carry; the structure should express that difference rather than adding chrome on top.
4. **Assume the title is not enough.** Legislation and division titles are frequently opaque;
   the card must give a non-expert a second route to comprehension.
5. **Phone-first, scan-first.** Short sessions, thumb reach, and glanceability outrank density
   or completeness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Material 3 colour roles throughout; light, dark, and AMOLED schemes are all first-class.
- Typography uses `sp` and Material type roles so system font scaling works.
- Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning (vote outcomes also carry numeric counts and
  text labels).
